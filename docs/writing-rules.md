# Writing rules

The design rules stop a surface from *looking* generated. These stop it from
*reading* generated — which is the harder problem, because the tell is almost
never one line. It is the same line-shape repeated five times.

**Check these across the whole set, not slide by slide.** A carousel can pass
four individual reviews and still read as machine-written the moment you read it
straight through.

---

## The bans

### 1 · The antithesis pair

No `X is A. Y is B.` / `Not X. Y.` / `It's not X, it's Y.` / `X, never Y.`

Two short clauses set against each other is the single strongest AI-and-LinkedIn
tell there is. It *feels* like insight because it has the rhythm of insight.

> ~~"A mark is an opinion. A payment is a receipt."~~
> ~~"A schedule is a promise. Not a spreadsheet."~~
> ~~"Speed is a feature, never a strategy."~~

**Allowance:** at most **one** per post or carousel, only in the opening hook,
and only when both halves carry real information.

> ✅ `"The dashboard says 94% on time. The shipper has seen 71%."` — two facts.
> ❌ `"A mark is an opinion. A payment is a receipt."` — two poses.

**Never** in a footer, a payoff strip, or a closing line.

### 2 · Aphorisms

Don't write lines built to be quoted. **If it would work on a poster with no
context, it says nothing.** Delete it and state the fact instead.

### 3 · Punch fragments

`Always.` `By hand.` `Every time.` `Nothing else.` `Simple as that.`

Verbless fragments used for rhythm. Write the sentence.

### 4 · Restating one idea three ways

**One claim per surface, stated once.** Not in the headline, again in the
subhead, and again as a closing flourish. Three restatements of one idea is
padding, and padding is what reads as AI.

### 5 · Rhetorical questions

At most one per post, and it must be answered on the same surface. Never
`…, exactly?` / `…, right?` / `Here's why.` / `Sound familiar?`

### 6 · Banned phrases

*here's the thing · let that sink in · make no mistake · simply put · that's the
whole point · quietly · spoiler · plot twist · the reality is · at the end of the
day · game changer · unlock · seamless · effortlessly · in today's fast-paced ·
it's no secret · look no further · dive in · we've all been there*

Plus whatever your own brand bans in `brand-kit/BRAND.md`.

## The requirements

### Footers and payoff strips carry information, not attitude

The closing line must tell the reader something they did not know a second ago —
a number, a mechanic, a consequence, who does it. It is not somewhere to restate
the headline with flair.

**Test:** does this line add a fact? If no, rewrite it.

### Vary the sentence shape across the set

Read all the surfaces in order, out loud if you can. If consecutive headlines or
footers share a grammatical pattern, rewrite until they don't. Same word used as
a rhetorical device on three surfaces: rewrite.

### Prefer the mechanic to the metaphor

A number, an actor, or a sequence beats an image every time. If a line could
describe any industry, it is not about yours.

## Caption rules

The caption is the text under the image, in the composer. **It is not a
transcript**, and its length is *inverse* to how much the surface already says.

- **Carousels: 90 words maximum.** The slides already made the argument in
  sequence. The caption's whole job is to earn the first swipe: hook, one fact
  that is *not* on any slide, your line, the question, the URL. That's the shape.
  A caption that re-narrates the slides makes the reader read the post twice and
  swipe once.
- **Single cards and videos may run longer (~150 words).** One surface holds one
  idea, so the rest of the argument has to live in the caption.
- **Never re-list what the slides enumerate.** If slide 3 names three things, the
  caption does not name the three things.
- **Keep the closing question and the URL** on every post.

### Never hard-wrap a caption

Write each paragraph as **one single long line**, with a blank line between
paragraphs, and let the platform wrap it.

LinkedIn (and most composers) preserve every newline you paste. A file wrapped at
75 characters posts with sentences snapped mid-clause:

```
two carriers can hold measurably
different pickup windows
```

The caption file should look wrong in a narrow editor and right in the composer.
No trailing spaces, exactly one blank line between paragraphs, never two.

**The exception:** a deliberate list where each item is its own line (`→` bullets
in a share kit) is correct and stays. What's banned is a *sentence* broken across
lines, not every newline.

### Published captions are a record, not a draft

**These rules apply to the next post, never to a published one.** A caption file
for something already posted is the record of what went out. Don't rewrite it to
meet a newer rule — the file would then disagree with the live post and confuse
anyone comparing the two.

---

## The calibration trap

Watch for this pattern, because it is nearly universal and it is backwards:

> In one real studio's first quarter, the carousels ran **198 / 174 / 172** words
> while the single cards ran **103–126**.

The posts with the *most* surface carried the *longest* captions. Carousels
should be the shortest captions in the set. Count the words before you call a
caption done.
